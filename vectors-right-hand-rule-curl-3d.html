The Right-Hand Rule for a × b: Curl the Fingers from a to b

This simulation shows the curl form of the right-hand rule for the cross product, with a right hand you can turn around by dragging.

To find the direction of `vec a xx vec b`:

1. Point the fingers of your right hand along the first vector, `vec a`.
2. Curl the fingers toward the second vector, `vec b`, through the smaller angle `theta` between them (less than 180°).
3. Your thumb, sticking out, points along `vec a xx vec b`.

The cross product is perpendicular to both vectors, and its size is the area of the parallelogram they span:

`|vec a xx vec b| = a b sin theta`,   `vec a xx vec b = (a_y b_z - a_z b_y, a_z b_x - a_x b_z, a_x b_y - a_y b_x)`.

Order matters: to find `vec b xx vec a`, start with the fingers along `vec b` and curl toward `vec a`. The curl goes the other way, the hand turns over, and the thumb points the opposite way: `vec b xx vec a = - vec a xx vec b`. When the vectors are parallel or opposite, there is no single plane to curl in, and the cross product is zero.

This is the same rule as the one for rotations: curling the fingers with a rotation, the thumb gives the axis. Here the rotation is the one that turns `vec a` toward `vec b` the short way.
