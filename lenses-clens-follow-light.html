This simulation shows a convergent lens and three sets of rays (pink, blue and green) that leave different heights of the object. Each set of rays converges to its own point of the image, showing how every point of the object is imaged to one point of the image. The light-source slider shifts which points of the object are lit.

For a thin lens of focal length `f`, an object at the distance `p_o` in front of the lens forms an image at the distance `p_i` given by the thin-lens equation

`1/p_o + 1/p_i = 1/f`,

with the magnification

`m = h_i/h_o = -p_i/p_o`.

A positive `p_i` is a real image behind the lens, which can be caught on a screen; a negative `p_i` is a virtual image on the same side as the object, which can only be seen by looking back through the lens. A negative `m` means the image is inverted.

Following the rays shows why a real image is inverted: the rays from the top of the object arrive at the bottom of the image.

This simulation is useful for understanding how a lens maps the points of an object onto the points of an image.

Convergent Lenses

The simulation below shows a convergent double-convex lens at the middle of the screen. Two filled circles on the optical axis indicate the focal points; two open circles indicate twice the focal length. The object is a vertical arrow to the left of the lens. Three sets of rays (pink, blue, green) originate from different heights on the object, showing how rays from each point converge to form the image. Use the Light Source slider to shift which points are lit.
