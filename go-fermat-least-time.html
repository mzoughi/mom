Fermat's principle: light travels between two points along the path that takes the least time (more precisely, a path whose time does not change, to first order, when the path is varied a little). In a medium of index of refraction `n` light travels at `v = c/n`, so the time along a path is its optical length divided by `c`: `t = (n_1 L_1 + n_2 L_2)/c`.

Least time. Light goes from A, in medium 1, to B, in medium 2, crossing the boundary at P. Drag P: the time `t = L_1/v_1 + L_2/v_2` is smallest at one crossing point. There the path obeys Snell's law,

`(sin theta_1)/v_1 = (sin theta_2)/v_2`,   that is,  `n_1 sin theta_1 = n_2 sin theta_2`.

The straight line from A to B is shorter, but it spends more of its length in the slow medium: it is not the fastest.

The lifeguard. The same problem in everyday form: a lifeguard runs fast on sand and swims slowly in water. The fastest route enters the water closer to the swimmer than the straight line does, so that less of it is swum; the best entry point obeys the same law, `(sin theta_1)/v_"run" = (sin theta_2)/v_"swim"`. Race a ghost that takes the best path.

All paths race. Send out many paths at once, each crossing the boundary at a different point, and let a dot travel along each at the speed of its medium. The first to arrive follows Snell's law; near it, the arrival times hardly change, which is why the path is stationary. With a mirror instead of a boundary, the least-time path reflects at equal angles: the law of reflection.

Huygens. Huygens' principle: every point of a wavefront is the source of a small spherical wavelet, and a moment later the new wavefront is their envelope. In the air the wavelets all grow at `v_1`, so the plane front rebuilds itself and moves on. As the front reaches the boundary, each point it touches starts a wavelet in the water, growing only at `v_2`. While the end C of the front travels `CB = v_1 T` to the boundary, the wavelet from A grows to `AN = v_2 T`: the new front BN is turned, and `(sin theta_1)/(sin theta_2) = (CB)/(AN) = v_1/v_2`. The dashed line shows where the front would be if the light did not slow down.

Wavefronts. The wave picture: a beam of plane waves slows down as it enters the denser medium, its wavelength shrinks, `lambda_2 = lambda_1 n_1/n_2`, and the wavefronts turn. Huygens' construction shows why: while one end of a wavefront travels `h_1 = v_1 Delta t` to the boundary, the wavelet from the other end travels only `h_2 = v_2 Delta t` in the slower medium, so that `(sin theta_1)/(sin theta_2) = h_1/h_2 = v_1/v_2 = n_2/n_1`. Past the critical angle there is no transmitted wave: total internal reflection.
