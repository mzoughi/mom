This simulation shows two pulses, A and B, travelling toward each other, in three views: pulse A alone, pulse B alone, and the rope, which shows their sum. Each pulse can be upward or downward, smooth, triangular or rectangular, with its own amplitude and width.

At every point and every moment, the displacement of the rope is the sum of the displacements of the two pulses (the principle of superposition):

`y(x,t) = y_A(x,t) + y_B(x,t)`.

Comparing the three views point by point shows how the sum is built. Pulses on the same side add up (constructive interference); pulses on opposite sides subtract (destructive interference). When they have passed each other, each pulse goes on unchanged.
