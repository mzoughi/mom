This simulation shows a ballistic pendulum: a ball of mass `m` and speed `v` strikes a pendulum made of a rod and a catcher box, and stays in the box. The pendulum then swings up.

The collision is very short, so the angular momentum of the ball about the pivot just before the collision equals the angular momentum of the whole pendulum just after it:

`m v L = I_"tot" omega_0`.

The moment of inertia about the pivot adds the rod, the box (by the parallel-axis theorem) and the ball:

`I_"tot" = 1/3 M_"rod" L^2 + (1/12 M_"box" (w^2 + h^2) + M_"box" L^2) + m L^2`.

Kinetic energy is not conserved in this perfectly inelastic collision: the system's energy just after the impact, `1/2 I_"tot" omega_0^2`, is much less than the ball's `1/2 m v^2`. After the collision, energy is conserved during the swing, which sets how high the pendulum rises.

The readout follows each step: the moments of inertia, the momentum and angular momentum, `omega_0`, and the energies before and after. This simulation is useful for studying collisions, angular momentum, moment of inertia and energy.
