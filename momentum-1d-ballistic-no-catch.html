This simulation is a ballistic-pendulum lab with a spring gun. The gun fires a ball toward a pendulum made of a rod and a catcher box. The ball's speed is not given: it has to be measured, in two independent ways.

In catch mode the ball embeds in the box and the pendulum swings up. During the very short collision, angular momentum about the pivot is conserved (kinetic energy is not):

`m v r = I_"tot" omega_0`,

where `r` is the distance from the pivot to the ball's path and `I_"tot"` is the moment of inertia of the rod, the box and the ball about the pivot. During the swing that follows, mechanical energy is conserved, so the largest swing angle tells how fast the pendulum started to turn, and from that the speed of the ball.

In no-catch mode the ball passes through and falls to the floor, where a ruler measures how far it lands and how far it dropped. As a projectile launched horizontally,

`h = 1/2 g t^2`,   `x = v_0 t`,   so  `v_0 = x sqrt(g/(2h))`.

The spring gun is not perfectly repeatable (about ±8 %), so several shots should be recorded and averaged. Comparing the two methods shows how well conservation of angular momentum and of energy work together. This simulation is useful for studying collisions, angular momentum, energy conservation, projectile motion and experimental uncertainty.
