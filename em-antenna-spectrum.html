Propagation is along k̂ (fixed, world z), the antenna's broadside direction — real dipole antennas radiate in a donut-shaped pattern, but this sim shows only that one representative direction. B is drawn at the same visual height as E for clarity; in real SI units B = E/c is far too small to see next to E. Changing the band changes λ, its units, and the derived frequency f = c/λ — it does not change E₀ or the on-screen rendering scale, which always shows about 3 wavelengths for legibility (real wavelengths span 12+ orders of magnitude across the spectrum, so true-to-scale rendering isn't possible on one canvas).

  3. At any instant along the wave, is B ever parallel to E? What is the angle between them?
  4. Using `c=lambda f`, if you move the wavelength slider so λ is cut in half (within the same band), what happens to f?
  5. Which spectrum band has the longest wavelength? The shortest? The highest frequency?
  6. Switch to Visible and try a few different λ values. Does the E field's color change to match? Why is this a special feature of only this one band?
  7. Drag the view until you're looking straight down the propagation axis (along k̂). What do you see about E, B, and the antenna's own orientation from that viewpoint?
