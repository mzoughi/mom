This simulation shows the electric potential of three point charges as a relief map. The charges lie in a flat square 20 cm on a side; above every point of the square, the height of the surface is the electric potential there. The view can be turned and tilted, and the charges and a probe point P can be dragged on the map.

The potential of a set of point charges is the sum of their contributions, with their signs:

`V(x,y) = k sum_i q_i/r_i`,   `k = 8.99 xx 10^9 " N m"^2"/C"^2`,

where `r_i` is the distance from charge `i`. A positive charge raises a hill and a negative charge digs a pit. Since `V` grows without limit near a point charge, the heights are compressed (on a logarithmic scale) so that the spikes and the gentle slopes far away can both be seen; the height slider changes the scale.

The electric field is the downhill slope of the potential:

`vec E = -grad V`.

So the field lines run straight down the slopes, from the hills (positive charges) to the pits (negative charges), crossing the equipotential bands -- the contour lines of the map -- at right angles. Where the bands are close together the slope is steep and the field is strong. A positive test charge placed on the surface would roll downhill, like a ball, along a field line.

The readout gives the potential and the field at P, computed exactly from the formulas above.

This simulation is useful for studying electric potential, equipotentials, the relation between field and potential, and the superposition of point charges. It was inspired by Paul Falstad's 2D vector field applet.
