Part 2: Electromagnet - Hands on Activity:

Make a solenoid by wrapping a `approx 1.0\ "m"` wire around a `approx 10\ "cm"` section of a drinking straw.

  1. Try, without powering the coil (no battery) to pick up paper clips with the wire and nail electromagnet. Note what you have observed.
  2. Now connect a power supply and try to pick up paper clips with the wire and nail electromagnet. What happens now? Where is the electromagnet the strongest the side or the tip?
  3. Use the compass to identify the poles of the electromagnet.
  4. Reverse the direction of flow of current by reversing the connection to the power supply. Use the compass to identify the poles. How does the direction of current flow affect the polarity of the electromagnet? Can you use the right-hand rule to predict the poles?
  5. Increase the current through the coil. Does the increase in current affect the strength of the electromagnet? Be careful not to keep the coil connected to the power supply for longer than 30 seconds.
  6. Add a core to the coil bu inserting a nail into the straw. Does the coil still act as an electromagnet? Use the compass to test it. What is the role of the core then? Note that some part of the lab will deal with magnetic domains.
  7. Move the nail so that only half of it is inside the solenoid. Note how that has affected the magnetic field.
  8. Change the core to a non-magnetic core, the wooden skewer. Does it still affect the magnetic field due to the coil?
  1. What is the purpose of the iron nail in an electromagnet setup?
  2. Which of the following materials would make the strongest core for an electromagnet?
  3. What happens when you use a pencil as the core of an electromagnet instead of a nail?
  4. Why does an electromagnet with no core produce a weaker magnetic field?
  5. What does the right-hand rule help you determine in an electromagnet?
  6. When using the right-hand rule, what does your thumb represent?
  7. How can you find the north pole of an electromagnet using the right-hand rule?
  8. How does increasing the number of coils in an electromagnet affect its magnetic field?
  9. What happens to the strength of an electromagnet when the electric current is increased?
  10. What is a common problem when using large currents in an electromagnet?
  11. Why are superconductors ideal for making powerful electromagnets?
