This simulation sends a single pulse along a rope and watches it reflect at the far end. The far end can be fixed (tied to a wall) or free (a light ring that slides on a pole). The pulse can be sent upward or downward, and the amplitude, the tension and the mass per unit length of the rope can be changed.

On a real rope some energy is lost as the pulse travels, so its amplitude shrinks along the way. Here the amplitude decays exponentially with the distance travelled, `A(x) = A_0 e^(-gamma x)`, with an adjustable damping constant `gamma` (per metre).

A pulse on a rope travels at the speed

`v = sqrt(T/mu)`,

where `T` is the tension and `mu` the mass per unit length: a tighter or lighter rope carries the pulse faster. The speed does not depend on the amplitude.

At a fixed end, the rope cannot move, so the wall pulls back and the pulse comes back upside down (inverted). At a free end, the ring overshoots and the pulse comes back the same way up. Either way, the reflected pulse travels back at the same speed.

The calipers measure distances on the rope: the horizontal caliper gives how far the pulse has moved, and with the time, its speed; the vertical caliper gives its height.
