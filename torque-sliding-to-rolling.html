1. Velocities of three points. Every point of a wheel moves with the centre, at `v`, and also turns around the centre, at `omega R` on the rim, forward at the top and backward at the bottom. So the top moves at `v + omega R`, the centre at `v`, and the contact point at `v - omega R`. The wheel rolls without slipping when the contact point is momentarily at rest: `v = omega R`. Then the top moves at `2v`. If `v > omega R` the wheel slides forward on the ground; if `v < omega R` it spins.

2. Kinetic friction and its torque. A wheel thrown without spin slides: its contact point moves forward over the ground, so kinetic friction `f = mu_k m g` acts backward, at the contact point. That one force does two things. It slows the centre: `a = -f/m = -mu_k g`. And, because it acts at a distance `R` below the centre, it exerts a torque `tau = f R`, which spins the wheel up: `alpha = tau/I`. The slipping `v - omega R` shrinks; when it reaches zero, the contact point stops slipping, kinetic friction stops, and the wheel rolls on at constant speed.

3. What decides the rolling. With `I = c m R^2` (`c = 1/2` for a solid cylinder, `2/5` for a solid sphere, `1` for a thin cylindrical shell, `2/3` for a thin spherical shell), the wheel starts rolling after `t = v_0 c / (mu_k g (1 + c))`, at the speed `v = v_0 / (1 + c)`. The final speed depends only on the shape: not on the friction, the mass or the radius. Friction turns a fraction `c/(1 + c)` of the kinetic energy into heat; the rest is shared between translation and rotation.
