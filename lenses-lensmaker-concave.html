This simulation shows a beam of parallel rays passing through a divergent (diverging) lens that can be built with the sliders: the curvature of its left and right sides, its diameter, its thickness and its index of refraction.

A concave lens, thinner in the middle than at the edges, bends the rays away from the axis. They leave the lens spreading out, as if they came from a virtual focal point in front of it; its focal length is negative.

The focal length of a thin lens of index `n` in air, with surfaces of radii of curvature `R_1` (the side the light meets first) and `R_2`, is given by the lensmaker's equation

`1/f = (n - 1)(1/R_1 - 1/R_2)`.

The sign convention: a surface curving toward the incoming light has a positive radius. For a thick lens, the thickness `t` adds a correction, `1/f = (n - 1)[1/R_1 - 1/R_2 + ((n - 1) t)/(n R_1 R_2)]`.

A lens of the same index as its surroundings (`n = 1`) does not bend light at all; the larger the index, the stronger the lens.

This simulation is useful for studying how the shape and material of a lens set its focal length, and the difference between converging and diverging lenses.
