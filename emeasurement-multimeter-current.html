Uncertainties for digital multimeters (DMMs) are always listed as a percentage of the reading plus some integer multiple of the least significant digit (dgt) on the scale.

e.g.  ±(2.5% + 3·dgt)

So, for example, if we measure a voltage of 32.00 V on a scale that has the uncertainty listed above (where 0.01 V is the least significant digit on that scale), then the absolute uncertainty of our reading will be:

(32.00 × 0.025) = 0.8
+ (3 × 0.01) = 0.03
= 0.83 ⇒ ±0.8 V

This simulation shows a 3½-digit digital multimeter: its display shows up to 1999 counts, so each range can show readings up to (just under) its full scale, with a resolution of the full scale divided by 2000. On the 20 V range, for example, the meter reads up to 19.99 V, to the nearest 0.01 V.

To get the most precise reading, choose the smallest range that does not overload: a reading above the range shows OL, and a range much larger than the reading wastes digits. A 1.234 V source reads 1.234 on the 2 V range, but only 1.23 on the 20 V range and 1.2 on the 200 V range.

The uncertainty of a reading follows from the meter's specifications: ±(a percentage of the reading + a number of digits), where a digit is the resolution of the range in use: `Delta x = p/100 * x + n * "resolution"`. The specifications below list them for each function and range.

To measure voltage, the meter is connected across the source (probes in COM and VΩF). To measure current, it is connected in series, so that the current flows through it (COM and mA, or 10 A for the largest range). To measure resistance or capacitance, the component is disconnected from any circuit and connected across the probes.
