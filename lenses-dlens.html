This simulation shows how a divergent (diverging) lens forms an image. A diverging lens spreads the rays out, so they never meet behind the lens; traced backward they appear to come from a point on the same side as the object. That point is a virtual image. For a diverging lens the focal length is negative, `f = -|f|`.

For a thin lens of focal length `f`, an object at the distance `p_o` in front of the lens forms an image at the distance `p_i` given by the thin-lens equation

`1/p_o + 1/p_i = 1/f`,

with the magnification

`m = h_i/h_o = -p_i/p_o`.

A positive `p_i` is a real image behind the lens, which can be caught on a screen; a negative `p_i` is a virtual image on the same side as the object, which can only be seen by looking back through the lens. A negative `m` means the image is inverted.

For a diverging lens and a real object, `p_i` is always negative and `0 < m < 1`: the image is always virtual, upright and smaller than the object, and it lies between the lens and its focal point.

Three small views show the object as it is, what the eye sees from its position, and what appears on the screen. The object can be shown as the letter F or as a clown; neither looks the same when it is turned around, so the views show clearly whether the image is upright or upside down. When the screen or the eye is not at the right place, the picture is blurred.

This simulation is useful for studying diverging lenses, virtual images, and the sign conventions of the thin-lens equation.
