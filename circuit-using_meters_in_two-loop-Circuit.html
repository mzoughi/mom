The main supply drives a current through R1; after R1 the current splits between two branches, R2 and R3, which join again at the bottom. The R3 branch also contains a second supply (zone 12) whose voltage V2 can be set from −20 V to +20 V: its sign sets its polarity, and the + and − marks on the drawing follow it. With V2 = 0 it is just a wire. Zones 1, 3, 5, 8 and 11 carry a wire in the path of the current; zones 6, 7 and 10 are empty pads beside R1, R2 and R3.

Using the meters. An ammeter measures the current through it; it has almost no resistance, so it must be placed in series, in a zone that is part of the path the current follows (zones that carry a wire by default). A voltmeter measures the voltage between its two terminals; its resistance is very large, so it is connected in parallel, across a component, in an empty zone, with wires from its terminals to the two ends of that component. An ohmmeter sends its own small current to measure a resistance, so it only works on a component that is disconnected from every source: if a voltage is still present across it, it shows an error. Remove wires (for example the wire in zone 1) to isolate a resistor before measuring it.

Safety. If any current would exceed 5 A (a short circuit, for example an ammeter placed in parallel across the supply), the supply switches itself off and shows TRIPPED.

Controls. Everything can be done without the diagram: choose a meter and a zone in panel 1, or two points in panel 2. On the diagram, tap a zone number to place the chosen meter, or tap two points to connect or disconnect them. The meter readings are listed under the diagram and announced. “Bigger diagram” lets the diagram scroll sideways on a small screen, so the points are easier to tap.
