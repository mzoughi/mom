Mirror equation 1/p + 1/q = 1/f  |  f = R/2 (concave f > 0, convex f < 0, flat 1/f = 0)  |  m = −q/p  |  q > 0: real image in front, q < 0: virtual image behind

This simulation shows the image of a clown on a skateboard in a mirror that can be convex, flat or concave. Press Play and the clown rolls toward the mirror and back again; or place it with the slider, or drag it.

The mirror is set by `1/f`, the inverse of its focal length. A concave mirror (curved in toward the clown) has `f > 0`; a convex mirror (bulging out toward the clown) has `f < 0`; a flat mirror has `1/f = 0`, an infinite focal length. The focal length is half of the radius of curvature: `f = R/2`. Moving the slider through zero turns a convex mirror into a flat one and then into a concave one.

For an object at the distance `p` in front of the mirror, the image is at the distance `q` given by the mirror equation

`1/p + 1/q = 1/f`,

and its magnification is

`m = h_i/h_o = -q/p`.

A positive `q` is a real image in front of the mirror, where light really comes together; it is upside down (`m < 0`). A negative `q` is a virtual image behind the mirror, where the reflected light only seems to come from; it is upright (`m > 0`).

For a flat mirror, `1/f = 0` gives `q = -p` and `m = 1`: the image is virtual, upright, the same size, and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front. A convex mirror always gives a virtual, upright, smaller image, close behind the mirror. A concave mirror gives a real, inverted image when the object is beyond the focal point -- smaller beyond `C`, the same size at `C`, larger between `C` and `F` -- and a virtual, upright, enlarged image when the object is inside the focal point.

At the focal point itself, `1/q = 1/f - 1/p = 0`: the reflected rays leave parallel and the image is at infinity. As the clown rolls toward `F`, its real image grows without limit and rushes away; as it crosses `F`, the image reappears, huge, behind the mirror, and shrinks as the clown comes closer.

The principal rays from the top of the clown are drawn: the ray parallel to the axis, which reflects through `F` (or as if it came from `F`); the ray toward `F`, which reflects parallel to the axis; the ray to the center of the mirror, which reflects symmetrically; and the ray toward `C`, which comes straight back. They meet at the image, or seem to come from it.

This simulation is useful for studying plane and spherical mirrors, the mirror equation, magnification, real and virtual images, and what happens at the focal point.

  1. Press Plane mirror and Play. How does the image move as the clown rolls toward the mirror and back? Use `1/p + 1/q = 1/f` with `1/f = 0` to explain why `q = -p`.
  2. Press Convex and Play. Describe the image: where is it, is it upright or inverted, larger or smaller? Does that change as the clown moves?
  3. Press Concave. Move the clown from far away toward the mirror. Where is the image when the clown is beyond `C`, at `C`, between `C` and `F`, and inside `F`?
  4. Press At the focal point. Where is the image? Explain using the principal rays and the mirror equation.
  5. With a concave mirror, place the clown at 3 m. Calculate `q` and `m` and check them with the readout.
