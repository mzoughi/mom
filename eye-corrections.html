This simulation corrects the common defects of the eye. The eye is the Le Grand schematic eye, and the rays are traced exactly through every surface, so the focus -- on, in front of, or behind the retina -- is computed, not drawn.

A defect is measured by its refractive error: the power, in diopters, of the lens that would bring a distant object into focus. A myopic eye focuses too strongly (or is too long) and needs a negative, diverging lens; a hyperopic eye needs a positive, converging one. Lens powers add, so a correcting lens of power `P` simply changes the total power of the system so that the image falls on the retina.

Glasses sit about 12 mm in front of the cornea. Because of that distance, a lens in glasses needs a different power from a contact lens for the same eye. If the eye needs `K` diopters at the cornea, glasses at a distance `d` must have

`F = K/(1 + d K)`.

For a myope of `K = -4.0` D this gives about `-4.2` D in glasses; the stronger the prescription, the bigger the difference. Contact lenses sit on the cornea, so their power is `K` itself.

Astigmatism comes from a cornea curved more steeply in one direction than in the other. The two directions (meridians) focus at different depths, so no single spherical lens can correct both: the prescription adds a cylinder, a lens that has power in one meridian only. Choose which meridian the cross-section shows; the eye chart shows the blur that results from both.

Presbyopia is the loss of accommodation with age. Distant vision may be fine, but a book at 40 cm needs about 2.5 D more than the eye can give. Reading glasses add that power.

LASIK reshapes the cornea itself. A laser removes tissue under a thin flap: to correct myopia it flattens the center of the cornea, increasing its radius `R`; to correct hyperopia it steepens it. The front of the cornea has power `P = (n - 1)/R`, with `n = 1.377`. The depth of tissue removed across an optical zone of diameter `S` is roughly given by Munnerlyn's formula,

`t ~~ (S^2 D)/3`  (t in micrometres, S in mm, D in diopters),

about 12 micrometres per diopter for a 6 mm zone. The cornea is only about 550 micrometres thick, and enough of it must remain for the eye to stay strong, which limits how large a correction LASIK can safely make. The simulation computes the depth exactly from the shapes before and after.

The eye chart shows the blur at its angular size: about one minute of arc corresponds to normal vision (20/20), and the blur grows with the defocus and with the size of the pupil.
