Angular Momentum of Two Moving, Spinning Balls

a close-up of the table from above · drag O to move the reference point

This simulation shows the momentum and angular momentum of two balls moving on a frictionless table, each in its own half so that they never collide. Each ball moves in a straight line at constant velocity and can also spin about a vertical axis; the marker on each ball shows its rotation.

The momentum of each ball is `vec p = m vec v`; the total, `vec P = vec p_1 + vec p_2`, does not depend on where the origin is.

Angular momentum is measured about a chosen point O (drag it to move it). A ball moving in a straight line has an orbital angular momentum about O,

`L_"orb" = (vec r xx vec p)_z = m v r_(_|_)`,

where `vec r` runs from O to the ball and the lever arm `r_(_|_)` is the perpendicular distance from O to the ball's line of motion. As the ball moves, `vec r` keeps changing, but `r_(_|_)` does not -- so with no force on the ball, its orbital angular momentum stays constant. It is positive when the ball goes counterclockwise around O and negative clockwise. It is zero for a ball heading straight toward or away from O, however fast.

A spinning ball also has spin angular momentum about its own centre, `L_"spin" = I omega`, with `I = 2/5 m R^2` for a solid ball; it is the same about any point. The total angular momentum is the sum of all the parts:

`L = sum_i (m_i v_i r_(_|_ i) + I_i omega_i)`.

Unlike momentum, angular momentum depends on the choice of O: moving O changes the lever arms. Two balls can have angular momenta that add up, or cancel, depending on which way they go around O and which way they spin.
