This simulation shows a mass `m` on a horizontal spring of spring constant `k`, sliding without friction. Pulled aside and released, it moves in simple harmonic motion,

`x(t) = A cos(omega t + psi)`,   `omega = sqrt(k/m)`,   `T = 2 pi sqrt(m/k)`,

where `A` is the amplitude and `psi` the phase, which sets where in its cycle the motion starts. The period does not depend on the amplitude. A graph of `T^2` against `m` is a straight line through the origin with slope `4 pi^2/k`. The energy changes back and forth between the kinetic energy of the mass and the potential energy of the spring, `E = 1/2 m v^2 + 1/2 k x^2 = 1/2 k A^2`.

The rulers and the movable axes on the graph measure positions, amplitudes and times; the ⏲ button starts a timer to measure the period.

Data collection. Below the simulation, record runs with different settings. Each run keeps its settings, its period (timed with the ⏲ button over N oscillations, and measured from the motion itself) and its whole motion. Plot any quantity against time, or any two quantities against each other with one point per run, fit a straight line (optionally through the origin), or average the points. The table can be saved as a CSV file or copied into a spreadsheet.
