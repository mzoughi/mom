This simulation shows a diffraction grating used to separate colours. A grating has many slits a distance `d` apart; light of wavelength `lambda` is sent in the directions where

`d sin theta = n lambda`,   `n = 0, +-1, +-2, ...`

Every wavelength goes straight through in the zeroth order, `n = 0`, so white light stays white there. In the other orders each wavelength leaves at its own angle: longer wavelengths (red) are deviated more than shorter ones (violet), and white light is spread into a spectrum. Higher orders are spread more, and can overlap.

Choose the light sources: one colour or several, or white light. With colour addition on, overlapping colours add as real light does. The face-on view of the screen shows the spectra enlarged.
