How to use it. A small lamp sits inside an insulated calorimeter filled with water of mass m. Set the rated wattage (determines the lamp's cold resistance), the water mass, and the LVPS voltage. Press Play to run time forward: the ammeter and voltmeter read the lamp's electrical operating point, giving power P = V·I and cumulative electrical energy W = ∫V·I dt, while the thermocouple tracks the water's actual temperature rise, giving the heat it absorbed, Q = m·C·ΔT. Use Collect Data Point to log a snapshot (with thermocouple measurement noise) as you would in a real lab, then pick any two quantities for the X and Y axes below -- turn on Show Linear Fit where a straight-line relationship applies (e.g. Q vs. W, or T vs. t at constant power).

This simulation shows an incandescent lamp submerged in water inside an insulated calorimeter. The lamp's rated wattage, the mass of the water and the supply voltage can be set. As time runs, the ammeter and the voltmeter give the lamp's electrical operating point and the thermocouple gives the temperature of the water.

The electrical power delivered to the lamp, and the electrical energy delivered over time, are

`P = V I`,   `W = int V I dt`.

The heat absorbed by the water of mass `m` and specific heat `C` when its temperature rises by `Delta T` is

`Q = m C Delta T`.

Comparing `Q` with `W` shows how much of the electrical energy ends up as heat in the water. The lamp's resistance changes as its filament heats up, which is why the current and the power change over time. Data points can be collected to analyse the run.

This simulation is useful for studying electrical power and energy, Joule heating, specific heat, and conservation of energy.
