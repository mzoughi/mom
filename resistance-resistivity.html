For a quantity that should not change from one measurement to the next (for example the measured resistivity ρ = RA/L for one material), the average is the best value and the standard deviation shows the scatter. The standard error of the mean is s/√n.

A wire 100 cm long is mounted between two probes. One probe (black) is fixed at the start of the wire; the other (red, or orange in colour-blind mode) slides along it. Only the part between the probes, the effective length L, carries the current. A low-voltage power source (LVPS) drives the current through an ammeter (A) and the wire; a voltmeter (V) is connected directly across the two probes, so it reads the voltage across that part of the wire only.

Choose the material, the diameter d, the effective length L and the LVPS voltage, then record the ammeter and voltmeter readings. The resistance is R = V/I. For a wire, R = ρL/A, with A = π(d/2)² the cross-section. The readings carry a small random measurement error (about 1.5%). If the current would exceed 5 A, the ammeter shows OL (overload) and nothing can be recorded: lower the voltage, lengthen the wire, or choose a thinner wire.

The Graph & fit tab plots any recorded quantity against any other, with the best straight line y = mx + b, the uncertainties in the slope m and the intercept b (from the scatter of the points about the line), and R². At least five points are needed. Vary one quantity at a time: for example, R against L at fixed d and material has slope ρ/A. The Average tab gives the mean, the standard deviation and the standard error of any quantity. The data stay saved in this browser, also when answers are submitted.
