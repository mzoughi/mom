Spinning Faster: Angular Momentum Is Conserved

L = Iω stays the same: make I smaller, and ω grows

A spinning body keeps its angular momentum `L = I omega` unless an outside torque acts on it. The moment of inertia `I = sum m r^2` depends on how far the mass is from the axis. So when a spinning person pulls the arms in, `I` gets smaller and the angular speed `omega` grows, in the same proportion: `I_1 omega_1 = I_2 omega_2`.

The kinetic energy `K = 1/2 I omega^2 = L^2/(2I)` is not conserved: it increases when the arms are pulled in. The extra energy comes from the work the arms do, pulling the masses inward against their tendency to move in a straight line. Stretching the arms out again gives that energy back.

Letting go. If the person lets go of the dumbbells, they fly off along straight lines, tangent to their circles, carrying their own angular momentum with them. The person's angular speed does not change: nothing pushed or pulled them.

The skater starts a spin with the arms and the free leg out, then pulls them in (or raises the arms over the head), and spins several times faster. The diver leaves the board with a fixed angular momentum; in the air nothing can change it. Tucking (knees to chest) makes `I` about four times smaller than when straight, so the diver somersaults about four times faster, then opens up to slow down for a clean, head-first entry. The centre of mass follows the same parabola whatever the diver does.
