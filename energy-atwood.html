This simulation shows a two-pulley Atwood machine. Two pulleys of adjustable mass and radius sit at the same height, an adjustable distance apart; a string runs over both, with a mass hanging near each pulley. Each pulley has friction in its axle, so releasing the system may or may not set it in motion.

The heavier side starts to move only if the difference in weight can overcome the static friction of both pulleys:

`|m_B - m_A| g > (2 tau_(f,s))/R_p`.

Once moving, with two identical solid-disk pulleys (`I = 1/2 M_p R_p^2` each), the acceleration is

`a = (|m_B - m_A| g - 2 tau_(f,k)/R_p)/(m_A + m_B + M_p)`.

In terms of energy, the potential energy released as the masses move a distance `H` goes into the kinetic energy of the masses and the turning pulleys, and into the work done against friction:

`|m_B - m_A| g H = 1/2 (m_A + m_B + M_p) v^2 + (2 tau_(f,k) H)/R_p`.

Runs can be recorded and graphed to measure the friction in the pulleys. This simulation is useful for studying Newton's second law for connected objects, rotational inertia, friction torque and energy.
