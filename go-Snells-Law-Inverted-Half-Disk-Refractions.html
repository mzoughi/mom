This simulation shows a beam of light entering a half-disk (a semicircular block of index `n_2`) through its curved face and meeting its flat face from inside; the block is surrounded by a medium of index `n_1`. Two protractors, one over the block and one below the flat face, measure the angles from the normal.

Aimed at the centre of the flat face, the beam crosses the curved face along a radius, that is along the normal, and enters without bending. At the flat face it obeys Snell's law, from the block into the surrounding medium,

`n_2 sin theta_i = n_1 sin theta_t`,

and part of it is reflected at an angle equal to the angle of incidence. When the block has the higher index (`n_2 > n_1`), the transmitted beam bends away from the normal; at the critical angle, `sin theta_c = n_1/n_2`, it runs along the flat face, and beyond it all the light is reflected: total internal reflection.

Why the centre is special. Drag the aim point away from the centre. The beam no longer crosses the curved face along a radius: it is bent as it enters (the dashed line shows where it would have gone), so it meets the flat face somewhere else, and at a different angle. Only a beam aimed at the centre keeps the angle that was set, which is why measurements are made there. The light source and the protractors can be dragged; the protractors slide along the flat face.
