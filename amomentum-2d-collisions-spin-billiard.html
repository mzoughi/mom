Collisions with Spin: Angular Momentum and Rotational Energy

This simulation adds spin to a two-ball collision. Each ball can spin about a vertical axis, in either direction, at an angular speed `omega`; the marker on each ball shows its rotation. The balls are solid spheres of radius `R`, with moment of inertia `I = 2/5 m R^2`.

Where the balls touch, two impulses act. One pushes them apart along the line joining their centres, as in any collision (its size is set by the coefficient of restitution `e`). The other is friction, along the surfaces, which opposes the slipping of one surface over the other. That slipping comes both from the balls' sideways motion and from their spins: the contact points slide past each other at

`u = (vec v_1 - vec v_2) * hat t + R (omega_1 + omega_2)`.

The friction impulse is at most `mu` times the normal impulse (Coulomb friction); if it is large enough it stops the slipping. Because it acts at the edge of each ball, it changes both the balls' velocities and their spins. With smooth balls (`mu = 0`) the spins cannot change at all.

The impulses on the two balls are equal and opposite and act at the same point, so they cannot change the total momentum or the total angular momentum. About any fixed point, the angular momentum has two parts -- the orbital part of each ball moving past the point, and the spin part of each ball turning about its own centre:

`L = sum (vec r xx m vec v)_z + sum I omega`.

The orbital part of each ball is `(vec r xx m vec v)_z = m v r_(_|_)`, where `vec r` runs from the reference point O to the ball and `r_(_|_)` (the lever arm) is the perpendicular distance from O to the ball's line of motion; it counts as positive when the ball goes counterclockwise around O. Tick the box to see `vec r`, the line of motion and the lever arm of each ball.

The parts can trade -- a spinning ball can set the other moving sideways, or set it spinning -- but their sum stays the same. The kinetic energy likewise has a translational part, `sum 1/2 m v^2`, and a rotational part, `sum 1/2 I omega^2`; friction while slipping turns some of it into heat, even when the collision is elastic along the line of centres.

When the balls stick together, they move off as one rigid body: its centre of mass moves in a straight line, while the pair turns about it at the angular speed `Omega = L_(cm)/I_"total"`, where `I_"total" = sum (I + m d^2)` (the parallel-axis theorem) and `L_(cm)` is the angular momentum about the centre of mass.
