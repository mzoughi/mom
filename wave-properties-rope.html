This simulation shows a continuous transverse wave on a rope. The frequency, the amplitude, the tension and the mass per unit length of the rope can be changed, and tools measure the amplitude, the wavelength and distances on the rope.

The speed of the wave is set by the rope alone:

`v = sqrt(T/mu)`,

while the frequency `f` is set by the source. Together they fix the wavelength,

`lambda = v/f`,   and the period `T_p = 1/f`.

Changing the frequency changes the wavelength but not the speed. Changing the amplitude changes neither: it only makes the wave taller. Each point of the rope moves straight up and down -- perpendicular to the direction the wave travels -- which is what makes the wave transverse.

The timer, the tracked point and the calipers can be used to measure the period, the wavelength and the speed, and to check `v = f lambda`.
