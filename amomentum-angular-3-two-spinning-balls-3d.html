Angular Momentum in 3D: Two Moving, Spinning Balls

the table in 3D, angular momenta as vertical arrows · drag to turn the view

This simulation shows the same two balls as the two-dimensional version, but in three dimensions, so that angular momentum can be seen as what it really is: a vector. Drag the view to turn it.

The orbital angular momentum of each ball about the origin O is the cross product

`vec L_"orb" = vec r xx vec p`,

which is perpendicular to both `vec r` and `vec p`. Since both lie in the plane of the table, `vec L_"orb"` points straight up or straight down. Its direction follows the right-hand rule: curl the fingers of your right hand from `vec r` toward `vec p`, and your thumb points along `vec L`. A ball going counterclockwise around O (seen from above) has `vec L` pointing up; clockwise, down. Its length is `m v r_(_|_)`, which stays constant as the ball moves in a straight line, even though `vec r` changes.

A spinning ball has spin angular momentum `vec L_"spin" = I vec omega` along its axis of spin -- up for a ball spinning counterclockwise seen from above, by the same right-hand rule -- with `I = 2/5 m R^2`.

The total angular momentum is the vector sum of all these arrows: `vec L = sum_i (vec r_i xx vec p_i + I_i vec omega_i)`. Here they all point along the same vertical line, so they simply add or cancel.
