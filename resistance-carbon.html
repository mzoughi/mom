For a quantity that should not change from one measurement to the next (for example the measured resistivity ρ = RA/L for one material), the average is the best value and the standard deviation shows the scatter. The standard error of the mean is s/√n.

A graphite rod (a pencil lead) is held between two alligator clips, red on the left and black on the right, connected to a digital ohmmeter by two probe leads. Choose the pencil grade, the diameter d and the length L of the rod, and the length of the probe leads, then read the resistance on the ohmmeter.

The resistance of the rod is R = ρL/A, with A = π(d/2)² its cross-section and ρ the resistivity of the lead. The probe leads add a small resistance of their own, about 2 Ω per metre of lead: plot R against L and it shows up as the intercept. The reading carries a small random error (about 2%). Turn the range dial to the smallest range that does not show OL (overload).

Pencil leads are graphite (a conductor) mixed with clay (an insulator). Hard grades (H) contain more clay and have a larger resistivity; soft grades (B) contain more graphite and have a smaller resistivity. The rod is drawn darker for softer grades. Resistivities used (Ω·m): 4H 3.0×10⁻⁴, 2H 2.0×10⁻⁴, H 1.4×10⁻⁴, HB 1.0×10⁻⁴, B 7.0×10⁻⁵, 2B 6.0×10⁻⁵, 4B 4.5×10⁻⁵, 6B 3.5×10⁻⁵, 9B 3.0×10⁻⁵.

The Graph & fit tab plots any recorded quantity against any other, with the best straight line y = mx + b, the uncertainties in the slope m and the intercept b (from the scatter of the points about the line), and R². At least five points are needed. Vary one quantity at a time: for example, R against L at fixed grade and diameter has slope ρ/A and an intercept equal to the resistance of the leads. The Average tab gives the mean, the standard deviation and the standard error of any quantity. The data stay saved in this browser, also when answers are submitted.
