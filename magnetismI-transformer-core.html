This simulation explores the core of a transformer. An alternating current in the primary coil (`N_p` turns) makes an alternating magnetic flux in the core, which induces an emf in the secondary coil (`N_s` turns). For an ideal transformer

`V_s / V_p = N_s / N_p`.

Why iron? Its high permeability concentrates the flux in the core, so almost all of the primary's flux passes through the secondary, and only a small current is needed to magnetize it. With an air core, or an air gap in the iron, the coupling is weak and the primary current is large.

Why thin insulated sheets? The changing flux also induces eddy currents in the iron itself, which waste energy as heat. In a solid core they are large: the losses are huge and the field is pushed to the surface. Splitting the core into thin insulated sheets (laminations) confines the eddy currents, and the loss falls with the square of the sheet thickness. Too much current can also saturate the iron: the flux stops growing, and the primary current soars.
