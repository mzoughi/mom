This simulation shows how to measure with a ruler, and how the design of the ruler affects the measurement. Four rulers are available: transparent or opaque, with the scale starting flush at the edge or offset from it. Objects to measure include two disks, two rings, a rectangle and a box with a hollow center.

Drag the red dot (or use the X and Y sliders) to move the ruler, and the blue dot (or the angle slider) to rotate it. Line the scale up with the object and read the scale where the object's edges meet it. With a transparent ruler both edges can be seen through the ruler; with an opaque one the ruler has to be placed beside the object. With an offset scale the zero of the scale is not at the end of the ruler, so the end cannot be used as the starting point. Disk 2 and Ring 2 do not show their center, which makes finding the diameter harder.

In the best case the uncertainty of one reading is half of the smallest division of the ruler. A length found from two independent readings (one at each edge) has the uncertainty

`Delta D = sqrt(2) xx 1/2 xx "smallest division"`.

When lengths are multiplied, as for the area `A = W xx H` of the box, the relative uncertainties combine:

`(Delta A)/A = sqrt( ((Delta W)/W)^2 + ((Delta H)/H)^2 )`.

This simulation is useful for practicing measurements with a ruler, choosing a good measuring technique, and estimating and propagating uncertainties.

    Reminder: in the best case scenario, the uncertainty in making one reading with a ruler is equal to "half the smallest division of that ruler", and that when the measurement depends on two independent readings, the uncertainty is then `sqrt(2)\ xx` "half the smallest division of that ruler".

    The uncertainty in the best case scenario is the minimum value that one can choose. Normally, the scientists chooses larger values.
