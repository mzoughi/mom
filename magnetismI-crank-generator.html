This simulation shows a hand-crank generator: a coil of `N` turns turned in a magnetic field `B` by a crank and a belt. Turning the coil changes the flux through it and induces an emf,

`epsilon = N B A omega cos(phi)`,

where `A` is the area of the coil, `omega` its angular speed and `phi` its angle. Drag the red handle in circles to turn the crank.

With the switch open, no current flows and the crank turns easily. Close the switch, and the emf drives a current `I` through the bulb. A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field feels a torque, and by Lenz's law this torque always opposes the rotation: suddenly the crank is hard to turn. The work your hand does is exactly the electrical energy delivered, `P = tau omega = epsilon I`. A cold bulb filament has a low resistance, so the current surges when the switch first closes.

In AC mode the current reverses every half turn; in DC mode a commutator flips the connections every half turn, so the current in the bulb keeps one direction, while the coil and the effort at the crank are the same.
