This simulation powers a generator with hamsters. Each hamster runs in a wheel that turns, through a belt, a coil of `N` turns in a magnetic field `B`, inducing an emf

`epsilon = N B A g omega cos(phi)`,

where `g` is the gear ratio of the belt. A hamster pushes hardest when the wheel turns slowly; at full speed it can only just keep up.

With the switch open, the wheel spins freely. Close the switch, and the current that lights the bulb also makes a magnetic torque on the coil that opposes the rotation (Lenz's law): the hamsters have to work harder, and the wheel slows. The electrical power delivered comes from the hamsters' work. How many hamsters does it take to light a flashlight bulb? An LED needs less power, but conducts only above about 1.8 V, and only in one direction.
