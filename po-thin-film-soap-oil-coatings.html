Readout

This simulation shows the colours of a thin film of constant thickness `t` and index `n_2`, between a medium of index `n_1` above and `n_3` below. Light falling straight down is partly reflected at the top of the film (wave 1) and partly at its bottom (wave 2), which travels an extra optical path `2 n_2 t`. A reflection has a half-wave (`pi`) phase change when the light meets a medium of higher index.

One flip (a soap film in air, oil on water): the reflection is bright when `2 n_2 t = (m + 1/2) lambda` and dark when `2 n_2 t = m lambda`. No flip or two flips (a coating on glass whose index lies between air's and the glass's): bright when `2 n_2 t = m lambda`, dark when `2 n_2 t = (m + 1/2) lambda`. The transmitted light is always the opposite. In white light, each wavelength is reflected differently, and the film takes the colour of what it reflects best.

The simulation uses the exact result for all the reflections inside the film: `r = (r_12 + r_23 e^(i delta))/(1 + r_12 r_23 e^(i delta))`, with `r_12 = (n_1 - n_2)/(n_1 + n_2)`, `r_23 = (n_2 - n_3)/(n_2 + n_3)`, `delta = 4 pi n_2 t/lambda`, `R = |r|^2` and `T = 1 - R`.

Soap films drain: thin at the top, thicker below, so they show coloured bands; just before bursting the top becomes the "black film", far thinner than a wavelength, where wave 1 (flipped) and wave 2 (not) cancel. Oil on water is thicker in the middle of a drop and spreads into rings of colour. An anti-reflection coating is a quarter-wave layer, `t = lambda/(4 n_2)`: wave 2 comes back half a cycle late, and with `n_2 = sqrt(n_1 n_3)` the two reflected waves are equally strong and cancel completely. A layer of high index does the reverse: a high-reflection coating.
