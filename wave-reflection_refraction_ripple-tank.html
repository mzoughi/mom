This simulation shows plane water waves in a ripple tank crossing a straight boundary between two media where the waves travel at different speeds (for water waves, deep and shallow water). The angle of incidence, the frequency, the amplitude, the two speeds and the position of the boundary can be changed; the boundary and the ray point can be dragged.

At the boundary, part of the wave is reflected, at an angle equal to the angle of incidence, and part is transmitted (refracted). The frequency stays the same, so the wavelength changes with the speed, `lambda = v/f`, and the transmitted wave bends according to Snell's law:

`(sin theta_t)/(sin theta_i) = v_2/v_1`.

A wave entering a slower medium bends toward the normal and its wavelength shortens; entering a faster medium it bends away from the normal. When `v_2 > v_1` and the angle is large enough, there is no transmitted wave at all: total internal reflection.

The ray diagram shows the incident, reflected and transmitted directions, and the readout gives the angles, the speeds, the wavelengths and the reflection and transmission coefficients.
