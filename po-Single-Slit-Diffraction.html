This simulation shows the diffraction of light through a single narrow slit. Light of wavelength `lambda` passes through a slit of width `a` and falls on a screen a distance `L` away. Instead of a sharp image of the slit, the screen shows a broad bright central fringe with weaker fringes on either side.

Each point across the slit sends out its own wavelet. In the direction `theta`, the wavelets from the two halves of the slit cancel when

`a sin theta = m lambda`,   `m = +-1, +-2, ...`,

which gives the dark fringes. The intensity is `I = I_0 ((sin beta)/beta)^2` with `beta = (pi a sin theta)/lambda`. The central maximum is twice as wide as the others: on the screen its width is `Delta y ~~ (2 lambda L)/a`. A narrower slit or a longer wavelength spreads the light more.

The side view shows the slit and the screen; below it, the screen is seen face-on, enlarged, with the intensity graph aligned beneath it. The magnification buttons enlarge the intensity curve, to reveal the faint side fringes.
