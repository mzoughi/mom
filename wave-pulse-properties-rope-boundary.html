This simulation sends a single pulse along a rope made of two parts joined together: rope 1 and rope 2 can have different masses per unit length. When the pulse reaches the joint, part of it is reflected back and part of it is transmitted into the second rope.

The speed of the pulse in each rope is

`v = sqrt(T/mu)`,

so with the same tension, the pulse is slower in the heavier rope. Going from a light rope into a heavier one, the reflected pulse is inverted (the heavier rope acts almost like a wall); going from a heavy rope into a lighter one, the reflected pulse stays the same way up. The transmitted pulse is never inverted.

The heights of the reflected and transmitted pulses are given by the reflection and transmission coefficients, which depend on the speeds `v_1` and `v_2` in the two ropes:

`r = (v_2 - v_1)/(v_2 + v_1)`,   `t = (2 v_2)/(v_2 + v_1)`.

The calipers measure distances and heights on the rope.
