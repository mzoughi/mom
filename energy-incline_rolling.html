This simulation shows a wheeled block released from rest at an adjustable distance up an inclined track. The wheels make friction negligible, so the block rolls down under gravity alone.

Along the incline, the component of gravity `m g sin theta` is the only force, so the acceleration

`a = g sin theta`

does not depend on the mass. Starting from rest at a distance `d` along the track (a height `h = d sin theta`),

`v = a t`,   `s = d - 1/2 a t^2`,

and conservation of energy gives the speed at the bottom, `m g h = 1/2 m v^2`, so `v = sqrt(2 g h)`.

Runs can be recorded and graphed to find the acceleration from the data and compare it with `g sin theta`. This simulation is useful for studying motion with constant acceleration, inclined planes, and conservation of energy.
