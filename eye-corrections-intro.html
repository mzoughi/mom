Nearsighted, Farsighted, Presbyopic: Correcting the Eye with Lenses

what the eye sees

This simulation shows how glasses and contact lenses correct the three most common defects of focusing: nearsightedness (myopia), farsightedness (hyperopia) and the loss of focusing range with age (presbyopia).

An eye sees clearly between two distances. Its far point is the farthest distance at which it can focus with the lens relaxed; its near point is the closest, with the lens working as hard as it can. A normal eye has its far point at infinity and its near point at about 25 cm.

Nearsighted (myopia): the far point is too close. A diverging lens is chosen so that an object at infinity forms a virtual image at the far point. With the lens a distance `d` in front of the eye,

`d_(oL) = oo`,   `d_(iL) = -(d_(fp) - d)`,   `1/f = 1/d_(oL) + 1/d_(iL)`,   `P = 1/f`  (diopters, with `f` in metres).

Farsighted (hyperopia) and presbyopia: the near point is too far. A converging lens is chosen so that an object at the normal reading distance, 25 cm from the eye, forms a virtual image at the near point:

`d_(oL) = 25 " cm" - d`,   `d_(iL) = -(d_(np) - d)`,   `1/f = 1/d_(oL) + 1/d_(iL)`.

The image is virtual (on the same side as the object), so `d_(iL)` is negative; the eye then looks at that image, a distance `d_(oE)` away, which lies within its range. A farsighted eye differs from a presbyopic one: it must accommodate even for distant objects, so its lens is never fully relaxed, while a presbyopic eye can relax for far objects but can barely accommodate at all.

Glasses also change the apparent size of things: a diverging lens makes them look smaller, a converging lens larger. This is how you can tell what kind of glasses someone wears just by looking at their eyes through the lenses.

Inside the eye, the simulation uses the reduced eye (a single surface of 60 D with `n' = 1.336`): a nearsighted eye is slightly too long for its power, a farsighted one too short. The blur seen is about the pupil diameter (4 mm) times the focusing error in diopters, in radians.
