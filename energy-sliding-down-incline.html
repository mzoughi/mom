What's happening

A solid sphere (mass 100 g, radius 15 cm) sits at the top of a frictionless 30° ramp. No friction means no torque and no rotation — ω = 0 and KR = 0 for every shape. All gravitational PE converts entirely to translational KE. Press Slide to release it.

Frictionless incline: no torque, no rotation  |  All shapes slide identically  |  a = g sin θ  |  v = √(2gh)  |  ω = 0, KR = 0 always

This simulation shows an object sliding without friction down an inclined ramp. Its shape, mass, radius and the angle of the ramp can be changed.

Without friction, nothing makes the object turn: it slides without rotating, and all the potential energy it loses becomes translational kinetic energy:

`m g h = 1/2 m v^2`,   so  `v = sqrt(2 g h)`.

The final speed depends only on the height dropped, not on the mass, the shape or the angle of the ramp. The moment of inertia `I` of the shape is shown for comparison with the rolling simulation, where it does matter.

This simulation is useful for studying conservation of energy and comparing sliding with rolling.
