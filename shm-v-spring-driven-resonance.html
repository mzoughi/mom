A mass `m` hangs on a spring of stiffness `k`, with some damping. Left alone it oscillates at its natural frequency

`f_0 = 1/(2 pi) sqrt(k/m)`,

and the oscillation dies away. Here a motor moves the stand (the top of the spring) back and forth with a small amplitude `A_d` at a frequency `f` you choose. The spring pulls the mass according to the stretch between the stand and the mass: `m ddot x = -k (x - x_"stand") - b dot x`, with the damping `beta = b // 2m`.

At first the motion is irregular: the mass's own oscillation (dying away) mixes with the motion the drive forces on it. This is the transient. After a while only the forced motion is left, at the drive's frequency, with a steady amplitude

`X = (omega_0^2 A_d) / sqrt((omega_0^2 - omega^2)^2 + (2 beta omega)^2)`.

Driven slowly, the mass simply follows the stand (`X ~~ A_d`). Driven fast, it hardly moves. Driven near its natural frequency, each push arrives at the right moment and adds to the motion: the amplitude grows until the damping removes as much energy as the drive supplies. This is resonance; the smaller the damping, the taller and narrower the peak. The mass also lags behind the stand: by almost nothing at low frequency, a quarter cycle (90°) at resonance, and almost half a cycle (180°) at high frequency.

Record the steady amplitude at many frequencies to build the resonance curve; then compare with the theory curves. For the vertical spring, gravity only shifts the equilibrium position (by mg/k): it changes neither the natural frequency nor the resonance.

    The spring has `k = 10\ "N/m"`, the mass is `m = 1\ "kg"`, and the damping is `beta = 0.1\ "s"^-1`.

  1. Calculate the natural frequency of the spring and mass.
    `f_0 =\ ` _____ ` "Hz"`
  2. Set the drive frequency to about half of `f_0`. Wait until the motion is steady. How does the mass's amplitude compare with the stand's? Do they move together or opposite?
  3. Now set the drive frequency to about twice `f_0`. Describe the steady motion of the mass compared with the stand's.
  4. Record the steady amplitude for at least 10 drive frequencies, more of them near the peak. At which frequency is the amplitude largest?
    `f_"peak" =\ ` _____ ` "Hz"`
  5. How does that frequency compare with `f_0`? What is the largest amplitude, and how many times larger is it than the stand's?
  6. Tick "show the theory curves". Then double the damping, and describe how the peak changes (its height and its width).
  7. At the peak, by how many degrees does the mass lag behind the stand?
