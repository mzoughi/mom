  1. Turn on the horizontal caliper. Measure the distance between two consecutive compressions (the wavelength). With the medium and temperature fixed, how does increasing frequency `f` affect this wavelength?
  2. What equation relates wave speed `v`, frequency `f`, and wavelength `lambda`? Use the wave-data panel to check your answer numerically.
  3. Compare a few different gases at the same temperature. How does a gas's molar mass affect the speed of sound through it?
  4. For a fixed gas, how does increasing the temperature `T` affect the speed of sound?
  5. Compare the speed of sound in a gas, a liquid, and a solid. What general trend do you notice, and why might that be?
  6. Select "Vacuum" as the medium. What happens to the wave? Why does this make physical sense?
  7. Turn on the tracked-particle markers and let the animation run. Do the marked particles travel along with the wave, or do they stay near a fixed home position? Contrast this with what the compression coloring shows.
  8. Pause the animation and turn on the compression and rarefaction markers. How does the particle spacing and color differ at each type of point?
