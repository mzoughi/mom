Reading a Graduated Cylinder

THE CYLINDER
15.0 ml
Read at eye level. For a liquid that wets the glass, such as water, read the level at the bottom of the curved surface (the meniscus). Estimate between the smallest marks.

A graduated cylinder is drawn upright, partly filled with a liquid. Its scale is in milliliters (ml), from 0 at the bottom to 30 at the top, with numbers at the larger marks. The surface of the liquid is not flat: it curves down in the middle, forming a meniscus.

A red pointer line runs across the cylinder. Move it with the purple ring beside the cylinder, the slider, the buttons, or the arrow keys when the drawing has the focus (up and down: 0.1 ml; Page Up and Page Down: 1 ml). On a touch screen, drag the ring, or drag inside the magnified view for fine steps. The magnified view, on the right, shows the scale and the meniscus around the pointer enlarged.

Line the pointer up with the bottom of the meniscus, then read the liquid level from the scale, estimating between the marks. The button “Where is the pointer?” tells you whether the pointer is above, below, or at the bottom of the meniscus, for those who cannot see the drawing.
