Two waves of the same wavelength `lambda` reach a point P along different paths. What they do there depends only on how far out of step they arrive, and that depends on the path difference `Delta = r_2 - r_1`: every extra wavelength of path delays a wave by one full cycle, so

`phi = 2 pi Delta / lambda`.

If `Delta` is a whole number of wavelengths (`Delta = m lambda`), the crests arrive together: constructive interference, amplitude `2a`. If it is a whole number plus a half (`Delta = (m + 1/2) lambda`), a crest of one meets a trough of the other: destructive interference, the waves cancel. In general the amplitude is `A = 2 a |cos(phi/2)|` and the intensity `I = I_max cos^2(phi/2)`.

The scene marks the path difference as a line: an arc centred on P, of radius the shorter path, cuts the longer path; the rest of it is `Delta`. Below, both paths are unrolled side by side and lined up at P, with a tick at each wavelength: counting the ticks gives the path difference in wavelengths. At P, the two waves and their sum are drawn against time, and as phasors: arrows that turn once per period, at an angle `phi` to each other; their sum is the resultant.

The zoom view magnifies the extra stretch of the longer path, with a ruler in quarters of a wavelength. Where it ends (the dashed line), both waves have the same distance left to P: the fraction of a cycle the longer-path wave has done in that stretch is how far out of step the two arrive. Whole wavelengths do not matter; only the fraction left over does.

Through a second medium. In a medium of index `n` (where the wave is `n` times slower) the wavelength is `lambda/n`: more wavelengths fit in the same distance. What counts is the optical path `n L`, and the optical path difference sets the phase: `phi = 2 pi ((sum n L)_2 - (sum n L)_1)/lambda`. A slab of thickness `t` adds `(n - 1) t` of optical path.

Reflected off a second medium. The reflected wave seems to come from an image of the source behind the boundary. Its path is longer, and if the second medium is denser (slower, higher index) the reflection also flips the wave, adding half a wavelength: `phi = 2 pi Delta/lambda + pi`. Reflection from a less dense (faster) medium does not flip it: the flip happens when `n_2 > n_1`. A reflected wave is also weaker: at normal incidence its amplitude is `|r| = |n_1 - n_2|/(n_1 + n_2)` of the incoming one (a checkbox shows this), so the two waves cannot cancel completely.
