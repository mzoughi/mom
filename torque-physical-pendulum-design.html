Design a Physical Pendulum

a rod with disks and rings: where is the centre of mass, and what is the moment of inertia?

THE PENDULUMdrag a disk or a ring along the rod
The parts
Readout

A physical pendulum is built from a rod (always present) and any of four flat parts, all lying in the plane of the swing and centred on the rod: a solid disk, a disk with an off-centre circular hole, a thin ring, and a thick ring with an inner and an outer radius. Parts can sit at the same place on the rod; the smaller ones are drawn on top.

The centre of mass is the mass-weighted average of the parts' centres: `vec r_"cm" = (sum m_i vec r_i)/(sum m_i)`. A disk with a hole is a full disk minus the disk that was cut out: the hole removes a mass `M (r/R)^2`, and moves the centre of mass away from the hole. If the hole is off to the side, the centre of mass is off the rod, and the pendulum hangs tilted: in equilibrium, the centre of mass is always directly below the pivot.

The moment of inertia about the pivot adds up part by part, each with the parallel-axis theorem: `I = sum (I_"cm,i" + m_i d_i^2)`, where `d_i` is the distance from the part's centre to the pivot, and `I_"cm"` is `1/12 m L^2` for the rod, `1/2 m R^2` for a disk, `m R^2` for a thin ring and `1/2 m (R_1^2 + R_2^2)` for a thick ring. For small swings the period is

`T = 2 pi sqrt(I/(M g d))`,

with `M` the total mass and `d` the distance from the pivot to the centre of mass. Mass far from the pivot adds a lot to `I` (it counts as the square of the distance) but only a little to `M d`, so it lengthens the period.

The challenges. In the centre-of-mass tab, the rod lies across a pivot at a given balance point; change the parts' positions, masses and sizes (without adding or removing parts) until the centre of mass is right above the pivot. Then the gravitational torque about the pivot is zero, and the rod balances; otherwise it tips toward the side of the centre of mass. In the moment-of-inertia tab, choose any parts and settings to build a pendulum with the given period.
