Six rods of different lengths are clamped to a base, which a motor shakes back and forth, across the rods, at a frequency `f` you choose. Waves travel along each rod at a speed `v`, from the shaken end to the free end and back.

At most frequencies the reflected waves do not fit, and a rod barely moves. But the clamped end must be (almost) a node and the free end is an antinode, so a standing wave fits when the length holds an odd number of quarter wavelengths:

`L = lambda/4, (3 lambda)/4, (5 lambda)/4, ...`, so `f_n = (2n - 1) v/(4L)`.

At those frequencies each shake arrives in step with the wave coming back, the motion builds up, and the rod swings strongly: resonance. Each rod answers at its own frequencies only: the longest rod at the lowest. Its first resonance (`L = lambda/4`) has no node except at the base; the next (`L = 3 lambda/4`) has one more node along the rod, a third of the way from the free end; and so on. Damping limits how high the peaks grow and how long the motion takes to build up and to die away.

The model is a rod (or a string) carrying waves at a fixed speed. Real stiff reeds, which flex like diving boards, follow the same idea, but their frequencies go as `1/L^2` and their overtones are not odd multiples of the first. The displacements are exaggerated in the drawing.
