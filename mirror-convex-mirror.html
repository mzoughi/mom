This simulation shows a beam of rays parallel to the axis striking a convex mirror. The radius of curvature, the diameter of the mirror and the position of the beam can be changed.

A convex mirror spreads the rays out: after reflecting, they move apart as if they came from a single point behind the mirror, the virtual focal point `F`.

For rays close to the axis (paraxial rays), the focal length of a spherical mirror is half its radius of curvature:

`f = R/2`.

Rays far from the axis do not quite meet at the focal point: the wider the mirror compared with its radius, the more the reflected rays spread out near `F`. This is spherical aberration, which is why telescope mirrors are made parabolic instead of spherical.

This simulation is useful for studying convex mirrors, focal length, virtual foci and spherical aberration.
