This simulation shows a vernier caliper, an instrument for measuring lengths, outer diameters and inner widths to a tenth of a millimeter. The outside jaws hold an object such as a disk or a block; the inside jaws (the small prongs at the top) measure the inner width of a hollow object such as the box.

The main scale is marked in millimeters (0.1 cm). The sliding vernier scale has 10 divisions that together span 9 mm, so each vernier division is 0.09 cm, one hundredth of a centimeter shorter than a main-scale division. This difference is the least count of the caliper:

`"least count" = 0.1" cm" - 0.09" cm" = 0.01" cm"`.

To read the caliper, first read the main scale at the position of the vernier zero. Then find the vernier line that lines up best with any line of the main scale; its number `n` gives the hundredths of a centimeter:

`"reading" = "main scale reading" + n xx 0.01" cm"`.

Drag the red dot, or use the Vernier arm slider, to move the jaw. Choose no object to practice reading the scales with the annotated arrows, or choose the disk, the block or the box to measure. In the best case the uncertainty of a single reading is half of the least count, `+- 0.005" cm"`.

This simulation is useful for practicing the reading of a vernier scale, understanding how two slightly different scales give a finer reading than either one alone, and estimating the uncertainty of a measurement.
