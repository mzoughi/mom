THE STRUCTURE
Masses, load and cables

A tensegrity structure seems to float: its top piece does not rest on anything solid. It is held only by cables (or chains, or strings), and a cable can only pull, along its own length. It can never push. The pull of a cable is its tension, `T`.

The trick. In the first four structures there are two rigid pieces that never touch. The bottom piece has an arm that reaches up; the top piece has an arm that reaches down, below the end of the bottom arm. A centre cable joins the two ends, so the top piece hangs from the bottom piece. Hanging from one point, though, it would tip over. The corner cables (in the hanging hooks, the two hold-down chains) tie the top piece down to the bottom piece and keep it level.

Equilibrium of the top piece. The top piece does not move, so the forces on it balance and the torques on it balance:

`sum F_y = 0`:   `T_(centre) = W_(top) + W_(load) + T_A + T_B + ...`

`sum tau = 0` (about the end of the centre cable).

The centre cable pulls up. Everything else pulls down: the weight of the top piece, the weight of the load, and the corner cables. So the centre cable carries more than the weight it holds up. The corner cables provide the torques that keep the top piece from turning. Move the load off centre: it now exerts a torque, so the cables on its side pull harder and those on the other side pull less. Move it far enough and a cable goes slack, drawn sagging and grey, because it would have to push. Then the top piece tips.

The whole structure. The ground pushes up on the bottom piece. These normal forces add up to the total weight, `sum N = W_(top) + W_(bottom) + W_(load)`; the readout checks this. In the hanging hooks, the upper hook sticks out beyond the base: with a light base and a heavy block far to the left, the ground can no longer push up on the right side, and the whole structure tips over.

Tightening. The cables are made a little shorter than the gaps they span, so they are stretched and pull even before any load is added. Tightening them more raises every tension, but the balance of forces and torques stays the same.

Failure. A cable snaps when its tension is larger than its breaking strength. Its pull disappears, the forces no longer balance, and the structure moves: it may find a new balance or it may collapse.

The two last tabs are tensegrities made of struts (rods) that push and cables that pull, with no two struts touching. At every joint, the push of the strut and the pulls of the cables add up to zero. A strut fails by buckling (bending) when it is pushed too hard.

The SIM finds the balanced shape step by step. The motion shown while it settles or collapses is slowed down and is not the real motion. Cables are coloured from blue (small tension) to red (near breaking). The arrows on the top piece show the forces acting on it, with lengths proportional to the forces.
