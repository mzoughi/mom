Wave on a Slinky: Longitudinal vs. Transverse, Crossing a Boundary

Frequency:
`f:\ ` 3 Hz
Amplitude:
`A:\ ` 20 cm
Tension (transverse):
`T:\ ` 100 N
Spring const., slinky 1:
`K_1:\ ` 0.25 N/m
Mass, slinky 1:
`M_1:\ ` 0.6 kg
Spring const., slinky 2:
`K_2:\ ` 0.125 N/m
Mass, slinky 2:
`M_2:\ ` 1.2 kg
  1. Without changing `K_1, M_1, K_2, M_2`, switch between Longitudinal and Transverse mode. Does the speed ratio `v_2\/v_1` stay the same? Explain, using the two different speed formulas.
  2. In Transverse mode, how does the wavelength change crossing into slinky 2 when `M_2 > M_1`? Turn on the caliper and check `v=f lambda` on each side.
  3. Longitudinal impedance is `Z=sqrt(K M)`; transverse impedance is `Z=mu v` with `mu=M\/L`. In each mode, how does the `Z_1`/`Z_2` mismatch affect the transmitted amplitude?
  4. In Longitudinal mode, set `K_2=2K_1` and `M_2=2M_1` (taping two identical slinkies together). The speed doesn't change — so why does the amplitude still drop at the junction?
  5. Switch to Transverse mode with the same `K_2=2K_1, M_2=2M_1` settings. Does the speed change this time? Why is tension the deciding factor here but not in Longitudinal mode?
  6. Set `K_1=K_2` and `M_1=M_2`. What happens to the wave crossing the (now invisible) boundary, in either mode?
  7. Turn on the tracked-coil markers in Transverse mode and let the animation run. Do the marked coils travel sideways with the wave, or only up and down?
  8. Compression and rarefaction markers are only offered in Longitudinal mode. Why doesn't a transverse wave on a slinky have compressions and rarefactions?
