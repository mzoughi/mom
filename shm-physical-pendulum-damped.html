This simulation shows a physical pendulum: a rigid rod of length `L` and mass `m`, swinging about a pivot that can be placed anywhere along it, with a ring (a hoop of mass `m_"ring"` and radius `a`, in the plane of the swing) fixed at any position on the rod. A damping term makes the swing die away: `theta(t) = A e^(-beta t) cos(omega t)`; light damping barely changes the period.

Unlike a simple pendulum, the mass is spread out. What matters is the moment of inertia about the pivot, `I`, and the distance `d` from the pivot to the centre of mass (marked CM). Using the parallel-axis theorem,

`I = 1/12 m L^2 + m (L/2 - p)^2 + m_"ring" (a^2 + (x - p)^2)`,

where `p` and `x` are the distances of the pivot and of the ring's centre from the top of the rod. For small angles the motion is simple harmonic, with

`T = 2 pi sqrt(I / (M g d))`, `M = m + m_"ring"`.

A physical pendulum swings like a simple pendulum of length `L_"eq" = I/(M d)`, the equivalent length. Moving the ring away from the pivot increases `I` more than `d`, and the period grows. Moving the pivot toward the centre of mass makes `d` small and the period long; with the pivot at the centre of mass, the rod does not oscillate at all. For the rod alone, `T^2 d = (4 pi^2 / g)(k^2 + d^2)`, where `k` is the radius of gyration about the centre of mass: plotting `T^2 d` against `d^2` gives `g` and `k`.

Layout. On the left, the pendulum hangs from a triangular pivot. On the right, a graph shows the angle `theta`, in degrees, against the time `t`, in seconds; the time axis grows as the run goes on. A bar at the bottom shows the time, and the timer reading once the timer is started.

Measuring tools (each can be shown or hidden): a protractor at the pivot, with an arm whose angle `alpha` is measured from the horizontal (90° is straight down); a horizontal y ruler, giving the vertical distance below the pivot in pixels; a vertical x ruler, giving the horizontal distance from the pivot in pixels (then `tan theta = x/y`); and two graph cursors, a horizontal one reading an angle and a vertical one reading a time.
