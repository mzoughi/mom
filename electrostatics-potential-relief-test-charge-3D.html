This simulation places a small test charge `q_t` in the electric potential of three fixed point charges. The potential is shown as a relief map: above every point of a 20 cm square, the height of the surface is the electric potential `V` there. Drag the fixed charges and the test charge on the map; set the test charge and its mass with the sliders; then release the test charge from rest and watch what the potential does to it.

The potential of the fixed charges is the sum of their contributions:

`V = k sum_i q_i/r_i`,   `vec E = -grad V`.

The test charge feels the force `vec F = q_t vec E` and, placed at a point where the potential is `V`, it has the electric potential energy

`U = q_t V`.

A positive test charge moves toward lower potential, down the slopes of the `V` map. A negative test charge does the opposite: it climbs toward higher potential. Switch the height to show `U` instead of `V`: for a negative test charge the whole landscape turns upside down, and the charge is again simply rolling downhill. Either way, a charge released from rest always moves toward lower potential energy.

No other force acts, so the total energy is conserved as the charge moves:

`E = U + K = q_t V + 1/2 m v^2 = "constant"`.

The bars show `U`, `K` and `E` as the charge moves: whatever potential energy it loses, it gains as kinetic energy. The motion is very fast at this scale, so it is shown in slow motion (1 s on the screen is 4 ms). The charge stops if it reaches one of the fixed charges or leaves the square.

This simulation is useful for studying electric potential and potential energy, the force on a charge in an electric field, and conservation of energy. The relief view was inspired by Paul Falstad's 2D vector field applet.
