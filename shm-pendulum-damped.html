This simulation shows a damped pendulum: the swing slowly dies away. The angle follows

`theta(t) = A e^(-beta t) cos(omega t)`,   `omega = sqrt(g/L)`,

where `beta` (per second) is the damping coefficient. Each swing is smaller than the one before by the same factor, so the peaks fall exponentially: `ln theta_"peak" = ln A - beta t` is a straight line in time, with slope `-beta`. The energy is lost to friction, and falls as `e^(-2 beta t)`.

The rulers, the protractor and the movable axes on the graph measure positions, angles, amplitudes and times; the ⏲ button starts a timer to measure the period.

Data collection. Below the simulation, record runs with different settings. Each run keeps its settings, its period (timed with the ⏲ button over N oscillations, and measured from the motion itself) and its whole motion. Plot any quantity against time, or any two quantities against each other with one point per run, fit a straight line (optionally through the origin), or average the points.
