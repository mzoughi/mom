This simulation shows a beam of light crossing a rectangular slab between two other media, so that it passes through three media with indices of refraction `n_1`, `n_2` and `n_3`. At each interface part of the light is refracted and part is reflected. Four movable protractors measure the angles at the interfaces.

At each interface the beam obeys Snell's law, with the angles measured from the normal. Since the interfaces are parallel, the angle leaving one interface is the angle arriving at the next, so

`n_1 sin theta_1 = n_2 sin theta_2 = n_3 sin theta_3`.

When the third medium is the same as the first (`n_3 = n_1`), the beam leaves the slab parallel to the incoming beam, only shifted sideways. Inside the slab, light reflected back and forth makes further, weaker beams.

When the light goes into a medium of lower index, total internal reflection happens beyond the critical angle `sin theta_c = n_"next"/n_"current"`.

This simulation is useful for studying Snell's law across several interfaces, reflection, and total internal reflection.

    The simulation shows a light beam that is aimed at one end of a rectangular slab of transparent material. Two movable protractors are provided; one to measure angles within the slab, the other to measure the angles of the beams emanating from the slab.
    Start with `n_2=1.50` for the index of refraction of the block and `n_1=1.00` for the index of refraction the medium outside the block. Set the angle of incidence of the beam at `i[1]=theta_i=30^@`.
