This simulation shows a push-pull scale: a spring that is compressed or stretched by an applied force. Each new force adds a point to the graph of the size of the spring force against the size of the displacement.

The spring pushes back with a force proportional to its displacement from its natural length, and opposite to it (Hooke's law):

`F_s = -k x`.

In equilibrium the spring force balances the applied force. A negative applied force pushes (compresses the spring); a positive one pulls (extends it). The graph of `|F|` against `|x|` is a straight line through the origin, and its slope is the spring constant `k`; the linear fit finds it from the data.

This simulation is useful for studying Hooke's law, springs, and finding a constant from the slope of a graph.
