This simulation drops a strong magnet down a metal tube. The falling magnet changes the flux through each ring of the tube, inducing currents that circulate around it: one way below the magnet, the other way above it. By Lenz's law these eddy currents oppose the motion, pushing up on the magnet with a drag force proportional to its speed,

`F = k v`.

The magnet speeds up until the drag balances its weight, then falls at a constant terminal speed,

`v_t = (M g)/k`,

much slower than in free fall. A better conductor, a thicker wall or a stronger magnet gives a larger `k` and a slower fall; a non-magnetic object, or a tube with a lengthwise slit that breaks the current loops, falls almost freely. At the terminal speed, all the potential energy the magnet loses becomes heat in the tube, `P = M g v_t`.
