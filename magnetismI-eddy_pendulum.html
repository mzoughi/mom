This simulation shows an eddy-current pendulum: a metal plate on a rod swings between the poles of a magnet. As the plate moves through the field, the changing flux induces currents that circulate inside the metal, eddy currents. By Lenz's law, their magnetic force opposes the motion: a drag that grows with the speed,

`F = -k v`,   `k prop sigma t B^2`,

where `sigma` is the conductivity of the plate and `t` its thickness. A solid copper or aluminium plate stops in a swing or two; a plastic plate, with no free charges, swings freely.

Cutting slits in the plate confines the currents to narrow fingers: the current loops are much smaller, and the braking almost disappears. The energy removed from the swing becomes heat in the plate, `I^2 R`.
