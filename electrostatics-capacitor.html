This simulation allows you to investigate the behavior of a parallel-plate capacitor, one of the most important systems in electrostatics. A capacitor stores electrical energy by separating positive and negative charge on two conductive plates.

The capacitance C of a parallel-plate capacitor depends on its geometry and the material between the plates, and is given by:

`C = kappa * varepsilon_o * A/d`

  • `A` = plate area (related to radius `R`)
  • `d` = distance between the plates
  • `kappa` = dielectric constant
  • `varepsilon_o` = permittivity of free space

In this simulation, you will:

  • Adjust the plate separation and plate size
  • Optionally insert a dielectric material
  • Measure capacitance using a virtual meter
  • Record and analyze data
  • Graph relationships such as `C` vs `1//d`

By systematically varying one parameter at a time, you will explore how capacitance depends on geometry and verify the expected proportional relationships.

Your aim in this question is to replicate the part of the experiment where you investigate the effect of the plate separation on the capacitance. Thus, the only parameter you should change is `d`, the separation of the plates.
