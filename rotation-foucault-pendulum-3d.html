THE PENDULUM

The pendulum

A Foucault pendulum is a heavy bob hanging from a very long cable, free to swing in any direction. It is set swinging back and forth along a straight line. Over the hours, the direction of the swing slowly turns: in museums, it knocks down, one by one, the pins set up in a ring around it. In 1851 Léon Foucault used it in the Panthéon in Paris to show that the Earth turns.

Why the swing turns. The only forces on the bob are its weight and the pull of the cable. Both lie in the plane of the swing, so nothing pushes the bob out of that plane: seen from space, the swing keeps its direction. It is the floor, carried by the turning Earth, that turns underneath it. Tab 2 shows this.

At the North Pole the floor simply turns once a day (once every 23 h 56 min, a sidereal day), counterclockwise seen from above. In the museum, the swing then appears to turn the other way, clockwise, once a day: 15° per hour.

At other latitudes only part of the Earth’s turning is a turning of the floor about the local vertical. The Earth’s rotation, `vec Omega`, points along its axis; at latitude `phi` its part along the local vertical is `Omega sin phi`. The swing turns at this rate:

`Omega_(swing) = Omega sin phi`,   `T_(turn) = (23.93 h)/(sin phi)`.

In Atlanta (latitude 33.7° N) the swing turns 8.4° per hour, once around in about 43 hours. In Paris, 11.3° per hour. At the equator, `sin phi = 0`: the swing does not turn at all. In the Southern Hemisphere it turns counterclockwise.

The swing itself. The period of one back-and-forth swing is that of any simple pendulum,

`T = 2 pi sqrt(L/g)`,

about 9 s for a 20 m cable. It does not depend on the latitude. The long cable makes the swing slow and lets it go on for hours; in museums, a small magnetic push at the top or bottom makes up for the air drag. The SIM leaves out air drag.

Speeding up the Earth. In real time the turning is too slow to watch, so the SIM can make the Earth turn faster than it really does, while the pendulum swings at its real pace. The readout gives the real Earth time that corresponds. Each end of the swing comes back to the ring of pins once per full swing, and the pins are 10° apart. If the swing turned more than that between two visits, it would skip pins, as a real pendulum on a fast-turning planet would. So the SIM limits the speed-up to keep the turn per swing below 7°, and says so on screen when it does. With a large speed-up the path on the floor shows small points at the ends of each swing: the bob was released from rest in the turning room, so it carries a little sideways motion. A real Foucault pendulum does the same, but the effect is far too small to see.
